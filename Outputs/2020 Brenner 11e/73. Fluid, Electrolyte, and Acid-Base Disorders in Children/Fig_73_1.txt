Fig. 73.1 Diagnostic approach to persistent hypokalemia in children. Transient hypokalemias are usually secondary to transcellular shifts of potassium ion. The diagnostic approach can be visualized as a flowchart: 1.  **Persistent hypokalemia** branches into two paths: *   Renal potassium conservation: This leads to "Lack of intake" or "Gastrointestinal losses". *   High urinary loss of potassium: This leads to evaluating Blood pressure. 2.  **Blood pressure** evaluation branches into two paths: *   Elevated: Evaluate Plasma renin activity (PRA) and Plasma aldosterone (Paldo). *   High PRA, Low Paldo -> Renal vascular hypertension, Coarctation of the aorta. *   Both high -> Primary hyperaldosteronism. *   Normal: Evaluate Acid-base status. *   Acidosis -> RTA type 1, RTA type 2, Diabetic ketoacidosis, Antibiotics. *   Alkalosis -> Bartter's syndrome, Gitelman's syndrome, EAST syndrome, Diuretics, Antibiotics. *   Both low -> Congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing's syndrome, Liddle's syndrome, Apparent mineralocorticoid excess. *EAST, Epilepsy, ataxia, sensorineural deafness, and tubulopathy; RTA, renal tubular acidosis.*
