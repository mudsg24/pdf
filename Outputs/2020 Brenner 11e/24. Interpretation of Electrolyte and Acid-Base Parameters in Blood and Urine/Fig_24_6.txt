Fig. 24.6 Buffering of H+ in the brain in a patient with metabolic acidemia and a contracted effective arterial blood volume (EABV). Top, Buffering of H+ in a patient with a normal EABV and thereby a low muscle venous PCO2. The vast majority of H+ removal occurs by the bicarbonate buffer system (BBS) in the interstitial space and in cells of skeletal muscles. Bottom, Buffering of an H+ load in a patient with a contracted EABV and thereby a high muscle venous PCO2 values. A high muscle venous PCO2 prevents H+ removal by muscle BBS. As a result, the degree of acidemia may become more pronounced, and more H+ may bind to proteins (PTN-H+) in other organs, including the brain. (From Kamel KS, Halperin ML. Fluid, Electrolyte, and Acid-Base Physiology; A Problem-Based Approach. ed 5. Philadelphia: Elsevier; 2017.)
