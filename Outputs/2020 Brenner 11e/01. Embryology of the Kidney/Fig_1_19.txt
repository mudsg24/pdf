Fig. 1.19 VEGF-A is essential for the development of the glomerular and peritubular capillaries. (A) Vegfa inactivation in podocytes leads to recruitment of fewer endothelial cells in the glomerulus which are subsequently lost. Podocytes and endothelial cells are stained with WT1 (green) and CD31 (red), respectively. (B) Transmission electron micrograph showing that in podocyte-specific Vegfa knockouts, the glomerular endothelium (en) lack fenestrae (arrowheads) and eventually detach leaving the glomerular basement membrane bare (arrow). (C) Vegfa ablation in renal tubules leads to a significant reduction in peritubular capillaries (stained brown for CD34). VR, Vasa recta. Panels (A) and (B) were adapted from Eremina V, et al. Glomerular-specific alterations of VEGF-A expression lead to distinct congenital and acquired renal diseases. J Clin Invest. 2003;111:707-716. Panel (C) adapted from Dimke H. Tubulovascular cross-talk by vascular endothelial growth factor A maintains peritubular microvasculature in kidney. J Am Soc Nephrol. 2014;26:1027-1038.
