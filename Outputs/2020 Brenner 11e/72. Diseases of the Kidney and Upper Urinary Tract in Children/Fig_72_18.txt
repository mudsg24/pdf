Fig. 72.18 Effect of intensified blood pressure control on renal survival in children with chronic kidney disease. Within 5 years, 30% of patients randomly assigned to intensive control with a low-normal, 24-hour blood pressure target reached the endpoint of 50% glomerular filtration rate loss or end-stage kidney disease, compared with 42% of patients assigned to standard control with a conventional blood pressure target. (Data from Wühl E, Trivelli A, Matteucci MC, et al. Strict blood pressure control and progression of renal failure in children. N Engl J Med. 2009;361:1639-1650.) (A Kaplan-Meier curve comparing intensified vs. conventional blood pressure control. The intensified control group shows a lower percentage of patients progressing to the study endpoint over 5 years, with a hazard ratio of 0.65, indicating better renal survival.)
