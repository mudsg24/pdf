Fig. 44.7 Cross section of mammalian kidney and schematic representation of renal tubular phosphate reabsorption in segments of the proximal tubule (S1, S2, and S3) through sodium-phosphate cotransporters. This task is accomplished by two distinct families of sodium-dependent phosphate transporters, one being NaPi-lla/NaPi-Ilc and the other PiT-2, which are expressed in the luminal membrane of proximal tubular cells. The number of sodium-phosphate cotransporter units of NPT2a/c expressed at the membrane is regulated by parathyroid hormone (PTH) and fibroblast growth factor-23 (FGF-23). NaPi-lla and NaPi-llc prefer transport divalent P₁ (HPO₄²), whereas PiT-2 prefers monovalent P₁ (H₂PO₄). The basolateral exit pathway remains unknown. The basolaterally localized Nat-K+-ATPase maintains an inwardly directed Nat gradient to drive cotransport. ATPase, Adenosine triphosphatase. (Modified from Biber J, Hernando N, Forster I. Phosphate transporters and their function. Annu Rev Physiol. 2013;75:535-550.)
