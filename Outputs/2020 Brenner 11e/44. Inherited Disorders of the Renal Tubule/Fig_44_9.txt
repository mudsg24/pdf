Fig. 44.9 Mechanisms of renal acidification in the collecting tubule. Dominant distal renal tubular acidosis (RTA) is caused by mutations in the gene SLC4A1 encoding the chloride-bicarbonate exchanger AE1. The AE1 gene (chromosome 17) encodes both the erythroid (eAE1) and the kidney (kAE1) isoforms of the band 3 protein. Mutations in the gene encoding the β₁-subunit of H+-ATPase (ATP6V1B1; chromosome 2p13) cause recessive distal renal tubular acidosis with sensorineural deafness. Distal RTA with preserved hearing is secondary to mutations in AIP6V0A4, which encodes the 24 subunit of the proton pump. Both H+ and H+-K+-ATPases are represented. The H+-ATPase is schematically represented according to the proposed structure of the F-type F₁-ATPase of the inner mitochondrial membrane. F₁ is represented as a flattened sphere 80 Å high and 100 Å across. The three a- and three β-subunits are arranged alternately like the segments of an orange around a central a-helix 90 Å long. Mutations in the β-subunit cause autosomal recessive distal RTA. Autosomal recessive distal RTA has also been found, in small kindred, for the SLC4A1 mutation G701D. ADP, Adenosine diphosphate; ATP, adenosine triphosphate; ATPase, Adenosine triphosphatase; RTA, renal tubular acidosis.
