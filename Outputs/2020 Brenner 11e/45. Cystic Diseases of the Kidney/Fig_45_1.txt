Fig. 45.1 Evolution of cysts from renal tubules. Abnormal proliferation of tubule epithelium begins in a single cell after a "second-hit" process disables the function of the normal allele or if the level of functional polycystin falls below a specific threshold. Repeated cycles of cell proliferation lead to expansion of the tubule wall into a cyst. The cystic epithelium is associated with thickening of the adjacent tubule basement membrane and with an influx of inflammatory cells into the interstitium. The cystic segment eventually separates from the original tubule, and net epithelial fluid secretion contributes to the accumulation of liquid within the cyst cavity.
