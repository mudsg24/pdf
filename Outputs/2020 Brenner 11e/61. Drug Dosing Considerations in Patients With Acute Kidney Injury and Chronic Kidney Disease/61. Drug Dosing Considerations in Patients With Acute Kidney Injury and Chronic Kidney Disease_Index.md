## Figures

### Fig_61_1

![Fig_61_1](Fig_61_1.png)

Fig. 61.1 Distribution and elimination of a drug after intravenous administration. An image of a graph showing the plasma concentration of a drug over time after intravenous administration. The y-axis is Plasma concentration on a logarithmic scale from 0.1 to 100. The x-axis is Time from 0 to 12. The curve starts high, drops rapidly in the "Distribution" phase (from time 0 to about 2), and then declines more slowly and linearly on the log scale in the "Elimination" phase (from time 2 to 12). A "Half-life" is indicated during the elimination phase.

### Fig_61_3

![Fig_61_3](Fig_61_3.png)

Fig. 61.3 Threshold concentration, CE05, producing 5% of the maximum effect and ceiling concentration, CE95, producing 95% of the maximum effect. With a Hill coefficient of H = 1.0, CE05 = 0.5 and CE95 = 190, whereas for H = 4.0, the threshold is higher, with CE05 = 6.0, but the ceiling is much less, with CE95 = 21 mg/L.

### Fig_61_4

![Fig_61_4](Fig_61_4.png)

Fig. 61.4 Although the average steady-state concentrations (Cave) are identical regardless of which dosage adjustment strategy one decides to use, the concentration-time profile will be markedly different if one changes the dose and maintains the dosing interval (t) constant (Scenario A), versus changing the dosing interval and maintaining the dose constant (Scenario B), or changing both (Scenario C). An image of a graph showing three different drug concentration profiles over time for three dosing scenarios (A, B, C). All three scenarios result in the same average steady-state concentration (Cave), indicated by a dashed line. Scenario A shows frequent, small peaks and troughs. Scenario B shows infrequent, very high peaks and very low troughs. Scenario C is intermediate between A and B. A table indicates the dose, interval (T), Cmax, Cmin, and Cave for each scenario.

### Fig_61_5

![Fig_61_5](Fig_61_5.png)

Fig. 61.5 Supplementary dose after hemodialysis (HD). To maintain therapeutic target concentrations, a supplementary dose must be given after hemodialysis to replace the removed fraction of the dose. The dose after dialysis (DHD) combines both the adjusted maintenance dose (Dfail) and the supplementary dose (Dsuppl).
