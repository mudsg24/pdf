![Fig_21_2](Fig_21_2.png)

Fig. 21.2 Fetal programming of hypertension in low-birth-weight rats (induced by maternal low-protein diet). (A) A bar chart shows the total number of glomeruli per kidney at 8 weeks of age in male and female rats from low-protein (LP) and control (CTR) pregnancies. In males, the LP group had significantly fewer glomeruli than the CTR group (P < .001). In females, the LP group also had significantly fewer glomeruli than the CTR group (P < .01). (B) A line graph shows systolic blood pressure throughout life in male (m) and female (f) offspring from low-birth-weight (LBW) and control groups. The LBW male and female rats had consistently higher systolic blood pressure from 2 to 10 months of age compared to their respective controls. LBW, Low birth weight. (Adapted from Vehaskari VM, Aviles DH, Manning J. Prenatal programming of adult hypertension in the rat. Kidney Int. 2001;59:238-245.)

### Fig_21_14

![Fig_21_14](Fig_21_14.png)

Fig. 21.14 Potential differing mechanisms impacting developmental programming of blood pressure in (A) males, and (B) females. This figure shows two flowcharts. (A) In males, a developmental insult leads to decreased intrarenal RAS and a lower nephron number in the fetus. In adulthood, this results in increased reactive oxygen species, endothelin production, and testosterone-dependent increases in ACE/Ang II. This leads to sympathetic nervous system activation and renal sympathetic nerve activation, ultimately causing hypertension. (B) In females, a developmental insult leads to no change in intrarenal RAS and a normal or slightly lower nephron number in the fetus. In young adulthood, there is no change in endothelin production, increased antioxidants, and estrogen-dependent increases in ACE2/Ang (1-7), resulting in no change in blood pressure. However, with age, increased visceral adiposity and plasma leptin lead to sympathetic nervous system activation, renal sympathetic nerve activation, and a decline in renal function, ultimately causing hypertension. (From Ojeda NB, Intapad S, Alexander BT. Sex differences in the developmental programming of hypertension. Acta Physiol (Oxf). 2014;210:307-316.)

### Fig_21_15

![Fig_21_15](Fig_21_15.png)

Fig. 21.15 Diagram illustrating the concepts of metabolic capacity and metabolic load in relation to blood pressure. This diagram shows two cylinders. The first, labeled "Metabolic capacity," is determined by nephron number, which is positively correlated with birth weight. High birth weight corresponds to high capacity, and low birth weight to low capacity. The second cylinder, labeled "Metabolic load," is determined by height, lean mass, and fat mass. The diagram illustrates that a high load and low capacity are each predicted to increase blood pressure. (From Grijalva-Eternod CS, Lawlor DA, Wells JC. Testing a capacity-load model for hypertension: disentangling early and late growth effects on childhood blood pressure in a prospective birth cohort. PLoS One. 2013;8:e56078.)

### Fig_21_17

![Fig_21_17](Fig_21_17.png)

Fig. 21.17 Proposed mechanisms of fetal programming of hypertension and renal disease. This is a flowchart illustrating the pathways from pregnancy to adult disease. **Pregnancy:** Various factors (Maternal diabetes/GDM/obesity, Maternal development factors like LBW, societal factors, maternal health issues, and genetic polymorphisms) can lead to either high birth weight or growth restriction/low birth weight/prematurity. **Childhood/Fetal Development:** Growth restriction leads to a low nephron number and altered sodium transport, RAS, SNS, and vascular reactivity. High birth weight can also lead to similar outcomes. Postnatal "catch-up" growth can occur, leading to overweight/obesity. **Adulthood:** Overweight/obesity can lead to metabolic syndrome and ischemic heart disease. A low nephron number can lead to hypertension and salt sensitivity, as well as hyperfiltration and reduced renal functional reserve/GFR. Additional renal "hits" can exacerbate these issues. Hypertension and hyperfiltration can both lead to proteinuria, and ultimately all these pathways converge on glomerulosclerosis, CKD, and ESKD. Diabetes is also shown as an outcome in adulthood. CKD, Chronic kidney disease; ESKD, end-stage kidney disease; GDM, gestational diabetes mellitus; GFR, glomerulat filtration rate; LBW, low birth weight; RAS, renin-angiotensin system; SNS, sympathetic nervous system. (Adapted from Luyckx VA, Bertram JF, Brenner BM, et al. Effect of fetal and child health on kidney development and long-term risk of hypertension and kidney disease. Lancet 2013;382:273-283.)
