Fig. 3.14 Hydraulic and colloid osmotic pressure profiles along idealized glomerular capillaries in hydropenic and euvolemic rats. Val- ues shown are mean values derived from the studies shown in Fig. 3.13. The transcapillary hydraulic pressure gradient, ΔP, is equal to P − PT, and the transcapillary colloid osmotic pressure gradient, Δπ, is equal to π<sub>GC</sub> – π<sub>BS</sub>, where P<sub>GC</sub> and P<sub>BS</sub> are the hydraulic pressures in the glomer- ular capillary and the Bowman space, respectively, and π<sub>GC</sub> and π<sub>BS</sub> are the corresponding colloid osmotic pressures. Because the value of π<sub>BS</sub> is negligible, Δπ essentially equals π . P is the ultrafiltration pressure at any point. The area between the ΔP and Δπ curves represents the net ultrafiltration pressure, P . Left, Lines A and B represent two of the many possible profiles under conditions of filtration pressure equilibrium; line D represents disequilibrium and line C represents the linear Δπ profile. Right, Pressure profile after correction for surgical-induced loss of plasma volume depicts a small positive ΔP at the efferent level indicative of disequilibrium conditions. Q , Glomerular plasma flow; SNGFR, single-nephron glomerular filtration rate.
