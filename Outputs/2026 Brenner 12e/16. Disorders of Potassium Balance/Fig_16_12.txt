Fig. 16.12 Succinylcholine-induced efflux of potassium is in- creased in denervated muscle. In innervated muscle, succinylcho- line interacts with the entire plasma membrane but depolarizes only the junctional (α1, β1, δ, and ε—multicolored) acetylcholine receptors (AChRs); this leads to a modest, transient hyperkalemia. With dener- vation, there is a considerable upregulation of muscle AChRs, with increased extrajunctional AChRs (α1, β1, δ, and γ—multicolored) and acquisition of homomeric, neuronal-type α7-AChRs. Depolarization of denervated muscle leads to an exaggerated K<sup>+</sup> efflux, due to the upregulation and redistribution of these AChRs. In addition, choline generated from metabolism of succinylcholine maintains the depo- larization mediated via α7-AChRs, thus enhancing and prolonging the K<sup>+</sup> efflux after paralysis has subsided. (From Martyn JA, Richts- feld M. Succinylcholine-induced hyperkalemia in acquired pathologic states: etiologic factors and molecular mechanisms. Anesthesiology. 2006;104:158–169.)
