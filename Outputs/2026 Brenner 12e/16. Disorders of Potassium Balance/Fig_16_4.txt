Fig. 16.4 K<sup>+</sup> secretory pathways in principal cells of the con- necting segment (CNT) and cortical collecting duct (CCD). The absorption of Na<sup>+</sup> via the amiloride-sensitive epithelial sodium chan- nel (ENaC) generates a lumen-negative potential difference, which drives K<sup>+</sup> excretion through the apical secretory K<sup>+</sup> channel ROMK. Flow-dependent K<sup>+</sup> secretion is mediated by an apical voltage-gated, calcium-sensitive BK channel. Chloride-dependent, electroneutral K<sup>+</sup> secretion is likely mediated by a K<sup>+</sup>-Cl<sup>–</sup> cotransporter. Water transport in principal cells occurs via aquaporin-2 (Aqp-2) and aquaporins-3/4 (Aqp-3/4).
