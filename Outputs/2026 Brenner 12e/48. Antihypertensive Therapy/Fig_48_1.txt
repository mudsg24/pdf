Fig. 48.1 Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS)–effect site for each type of RAAS-blocking drugs. The two major classes of drugs that target the RAAS are the angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors and the selective AT1 receptor blockers (ARBs). Both these drug classes target angiotensin II, but the differences in their mechanisms of action have implications for their effects on other pathways and receptors. Both ACE inhibitors and ARBs are effective antihypertensive agents that have been shown to reduce the risk of cardiovascular and kidney events. Direct inhibition of renin—the most proximal aspect of the RAAS—became clinically feasible in 2007 with the introduction of aliskiren. AT, Angiotensin; DRI, direct renin inhibi- tor. (From Robles NR, Cerezo I, Hernandez-Gallego R. Renin-angiotensin system blocking drugs. J Cardiovasc Pharmacol Ther. 2014;19:14–33.)
