Fig. 49.19 Diagrammatic representation of mechanisms that increase (↑) K<sup>+</sup> excretion by the collecting ducts or partition K<sup>+</sup> into cells during therapy with a thiazide or loop diuretic, and strategies for prevention or treatment (dashed lines) with direct renin inhibitors, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs; e.g., spironolactone), epithe- lial Na<sup>+</sup> channel (ENaC) blockers (amiloride or triamterene), or KCl supplements. Diuretics stimulate the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), increase distal tubule flow and release of arginine vasopressin, and generate metabolic alkalosis, all of which enhance K<sup>+</sup> secretion in the collecting ducts. Two distinct mechanisms are responsible for K<sup>+</sup> secretion in the collecting ducts, one mediated by the renal outer medulla K<sup>+</sup> (ROMK) channels, and the other by big K<sup>+</sup> (BK) channels.
