| Table 41.5 KDIGO Practice Points for use of Renin-Angiotensin System Blockers in Patients with Diabetic Kidney Disease<sup>32</sup> ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy may be considered in patients with diabetes, albuminuria, and normal blood pressure. After starting or increasing the dose of an ACE inhibitor or ARB, monitor for changes in blood pressure, serum creatinine, and serum potassium within 2-4 weeks. ACE inhibitor/ARB therapy should be continued unless serum creatinine increases by more than 30% within 4 weeks of drug initiation or an increase in dose. Women receiving an ACE inhibitor or ARB should be advised about appropriate contraception and should be advised to discontinue therapy if they are considering pregnancy or become pregnant. Hyperkalemia associated with ACE inhibitor/ARB use can often be managed by interventions to reduce serum potassium levels without decreasing the dose or stopping the ACE inhibitor or ARB immediately. Reduce the dose or discontinue ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy with either symptomatic hypotension or uncontrolled hyperkalemia despite alternative medical treatment approaches, or to reduce uremic symptoms in the setting of kidney failure. •	 Do not use dual renin-angiotensin system blockade. ACE, Angiotensin-converting enzyme; ARB, angiotensin II receptor blocker; KDIGO,Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes. |
