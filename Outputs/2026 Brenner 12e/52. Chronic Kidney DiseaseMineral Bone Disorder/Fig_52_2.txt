Fig. 52.2 Epithelial calcium active transport. The late part of the distal convoluted tubule (DCT) and connecting tubule (CNT) play an im- portant role in fine-tuning renal excretion of Ca<sup>2+</sup>. The epithelial Ca<sup>2+</sup> channel (TRPV5) is primarily expressed apically in these segments and colocalizes with calbindin-D (28K), Na<sup>+</sup>/Ca<sup>2+</sup> exchanger (NCX1), and the plasma membrane adenosine triphosphatase (ATPase; PMCA1b). Upon entry via TRPV5, Ca<sup>2+</sup> is buffered by 28K and diffuses to the basolateral membrane, where it is released and extruded by a concerted action of NCX1 and PMCA1b. In addition, the basolateral membrane exposes a parathyroid hormone (PTH) receptor (PTHR) and the Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup>- ATPase consisting of the α, β, and γ subunits. PTHR activation by PTH stimulates TRPV5 activity and entered Ca<sup>2+</sup> can subsequently control the expression level of the Ca<sup>2+</sup> transporters. At the apical membrane, there is a bradykinin receptor (BK2) that is activated by urinary tissue kallikrein (TK) to activate TRPV5-mediated Ca<sup>2+</sup> influx. In the cell, entered Ca<sup>2+</sup> acts as negative feedback on channel activity, and 28K plays a regulatory role by association with TRPV5 under low intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> concentrations. Extracellular urinary klotho directly stimulates TRPV5 at the apical membrane by modification of the N-glycan, whereas intracellular klotho enhances Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase surface expression, which in turn activates NCX1-mediated Ca<sup>2+</sup> efflux. ADP, Adenosine diphosphate; DCT1, early part of distal convoluted tubule; PT, proximal tubule; TAL, thick ascending limb of Henle. (From Boros S, Bindels RJM, Hoenderop JGJ. Active Ca<sup>2+</sup> reabsorption in the connecting tubule. Pflügers Arch Eur J Physiol. 2009;458:99–109.)
