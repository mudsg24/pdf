Fig. 58.8 Relationship between podocyte dysfunction and glomerular endothelial cell distribution in preeclampsia. Schema summariz- ing changes in the glomerulus barrier in normal and preeclamptic pregnancy. (A) In a normal pregnancy, there is an excess of vascular endothelia growth factor VEGF (V shape), compared with the circulating fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFLT-1; black triangle) in women who will develop pre- eclampsia. (B) Placentally derived factors cause endothelial dysfunction, which does not have a direct effect on the podocytes. The circulating SF LT1 does bind to and inhibit the effect of the podocyte-produced VEGF thought to have a major role in maintaining a healthy glomerular barri- er. Dysfunctional endothelial cells produce endothelin-1 (ET, red circles), which in turn affects the podocytes and induces nephrin shedding (red rectangle). The placentally derived factors cause a reduction in endothelial cell fenestrations (straight lines). (Adapted from Hennessy A, Makris A. Preeclamptic nephropathy. Nephrology (Carlton). 2011;16[2]:134–143.)<sup>88</sup>
