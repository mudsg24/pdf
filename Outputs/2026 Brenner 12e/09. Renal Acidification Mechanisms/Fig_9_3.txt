Fig. 9.3 Bicarbonate transport by the A-cell and B-cell. Top panel, a model of acid-base transport by the type A intercalated cell. Two families of H<sup>+</sup> transporters, H<sup>+</sup>-ATPase and H<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase, are present in the apical plasma membrane. Secreted H<sup>+</sup> titrates luminal HCO <sup>–</sup> to form H CO , which dehydrates to water (H O) and CO . Cytosolic H<sup>+</sup> and HCO <sup>–</sup> are formed from CA II–accelerated hydration of CO to form H CO , which rapidly dissociates to H⁺ and HCO₃⁻. Cytosolic HCO <sup>–</sup> exits across the basolateral plasma membrane via the anion exchanger, kAE1. Cl<sup>–</sup> that enters via kAE1 recycles via a basolateral Cl<sup>–</sup> channel. K<sup>+</sup> that enters via apical H<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase can either recycle via an apical, Ba<sup>+</sup>-sensitive K<sup>+</sup> channel or the K-Cl cotransporter, KCC4. Bottom panel, H⁺/HCO₃<sup>–</sup> transport by the type B intercalated cell. Apical pendrin is the primary mechanism of bicarbonate secretion. Chloride enters the cell via pendrin and exits across a basolateral chloride channel, ClC-K2/ClC- Kb, resulting in Cl⁻ reabsorption coupled to HCO₃<sup>–</sup> secretion; recycles via apical CFTR, resulting in HCO₃<sup>–</sup> secretion without net Cl⁻ reabsorption; or couples with apical NDCBE to mediate net NaCl reabsorption without net HCO₃<sup>–</sup> transport. Basolateral H<sup>+</sup>-ATPase extrudes protons into the peritubular compartment. Cytoplasmic bicarbonate and protons are produced from CO and water in a CA II–catalyzed reaction. In addition, an apical H<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase is present. Basolateral AE4 is present and regulates net HCO₃<sup>–</sup> secretion via the regulation of apical pendrin.
