| Table 15.2  Systematic Method for Diagnosis of Simple and Mixed Acid-Base Disorders 1. Measure arterial blood gas and electrolyte concentrations simultaneously. 2. Determine whether the compensation is appropriate for a simple acid-base disorder; if inappropriate, a mixed acid- base disorder is present (see Table 15.1 and Fig. 15.5). 3. Calculate the anion gap (corrected for albumin) to determine the presence of a high AG metabolic acidosis: Appreciate the four major categories of high anion gap acidoses: ketoacidosis, lactic acidosis, renal failure acidosis, toxin- or poison-induced acidosis. Appreciate the two major causes of non–AG acidoses: gastrointestinal loss of HCO <sup>−</sup> and renal loss of HCO <sup>−</sup>. 4. Compare the relative changes of HCO <sup>−</sup> and AG (ΔHCO and ΔAG, respectively) to look for mixed disorders (see text). |
