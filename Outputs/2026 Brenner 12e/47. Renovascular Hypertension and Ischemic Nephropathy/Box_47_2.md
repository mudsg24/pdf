Box 47.2 Clinical features of patients with renovascular hypertension Syndromes associated with renovascular hypertension 1. Early- or late-onset hypertension (<30 years >50 years) 2. Acceleration of treated essential hypertension 3. Deterioration of renal function in treated essential hyper- tension 4. Acute renal failure during treatment of hypertension 5. “Flash” pulmonary edema 6. Progressive renal failure 7. Refractory congestive cardiac failure The above “syndromes” should alert the clinician to the pos- sible contribution of renovascular disease in a given patient. Numbers 5 to 7 are most common in patients with bilateral disease, many of whom are treated as “essential hypertension” until these characteristics appear.
