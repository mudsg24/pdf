Fig. e47.9 (A) Duplex ultrasound velocity measurement in a patient with high-grade renal artery stenosis affecting the proximal left renal artery (LRA PRX). Peak systolic velocities reach 605 cm/s (6.05 m/s), well above the normal upper limit of 180 cm/s. (B) Segmental arterial branch ul- trasound in the distal segmental renal arteries demonstrates “parvus” and “tardus” dampening of the signal characteristic of poststenotic wave- forms. The utility of these measurements depends on the ability to obtain reliable identification of vessel segments and the skills of the operator. Once the location of a vascular lesion is known, subsequent studies can be performed more easily to track progression of vascular occlusion, restenosis, and/or the results of endovascular intervention.
