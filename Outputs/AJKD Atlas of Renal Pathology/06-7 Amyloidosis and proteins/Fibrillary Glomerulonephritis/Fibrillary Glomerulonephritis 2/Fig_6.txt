Fig 6. Higher power shows randomly oriented ﬁbrils in the mesangium and glomerular basement membrane in ﬁbrillary glomerulonephritis. These ﬁbrils tend to be slightly larger than those in amyloid, although there is some overlap so that ﬁbril size alone cannot be used to deﬁnitively distinguish this from amyloid. A negative Congo red stain should be done to rule out amyloid deposits (transmission electron microscopy; original magniﬁcation 351,250).
