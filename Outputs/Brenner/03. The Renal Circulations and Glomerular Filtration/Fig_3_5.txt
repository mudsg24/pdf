Fig. 3.5 The arterial to venous (AV) oxygen shunt. Oxygen is delivered by red blood cells in the artery. Red blood cells release oxygen in the capillaries, which diffuses into the interstitium to reach target cells. Blood with low oxygen tension passes into the vein. The juxtaposition of the arteries and veins present in the kidney facilitates oxygen diffusion from the artery to the vein. Oxygen tensions in the capillaries are rela- tively low when red blood cells reach the peritubular capillary plexus, which indicate that the kidney is inefficient in extracting oxygen. The relative oxygen tensions are represented by the size of the circles surrounding O2. (From Mimura I, Nangaku M. The suffocating kidney: tubulointerstitial hypoxia in end-stage renal disease. Nat Rev Nephrol. 2010;6:667-678.)
