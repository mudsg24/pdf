Table 16.2 Systematic Method for Diagnosis of Simple and Mixed Acid-Base Disorders 1. Measure arterial blood gas and electrolyte concentrations simultaneously. 2. Determine whether the compensation is appropriate for a simple acid-base disorder; if inappropriate, a mixed acid-base disorder is present (see Table 16.1 and Fig. 16.5). 3. Calculate the anion gap (corrected for albumin) to determine the presence of a high AG metabolic acidosis. Appreciate the four major categories of high anion gap acidoses: ketoacidosis, lactic acidosis, renal failure acidosis, toxin- or poison-induced acidosis Appreciate the two major causes of non-AG acidoses: gastrointestinal loss of HCO3, renal loss of HCO3 4. Compare the relative changes of HCO3 and AG (AHCO3 and AAG, respectively) to look for mixed disorders (see text).
