Fig. 47.19 Outcome of revascularization as measured by mean arterial blood pressure and number of antihypertensive agents in 138 patients with renal artery stenosis. These patients were divided into groups with ultrasound-determined resistive index above 80 and those lower than 80 in the most severely affected kidney. The authors indicate that a high resistive index reflects intrinsic parenchymal and small vessel disease in the kidney that does not improve after revascularization. Those with lower indices had both lower blood pressures during follow-up and lower antihypertensive medication requirements. (From Radermacher J, Chavan A, Bleck J, et al. Use of Doppler ultrasonography to predict the outcome of therapy for renal-artery stenosis. N Engl J Med. 2001;344:410-417.)
