Fig. 53.13 Regulation of serum phosphorus levels. As phosphorus levels increase (or there is a long-term phosphorus load), levels of both parathyroid hormone (PTH) and fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF-23) are increased. Both of these elevations in turn increase urinary phosphate (Pi) excretion. The two hormones differ in respect to their effects on the vitamin D axis. PTH stimulates 1a-hydroxylase activity, thereby increasing the production of calcitriol, which in turn negatively feeds back on the parathyroid gland to decrease PTH secretion. By contrast, FGF-23 inhibits 1x-hydroxylase activity, thereby decreasing the production of calcitriol, thus feeding back to stimulate further secretion of FGF-23. FGF-23 and PTH also regulate each other. Finally, low calcium levels stimulate PTH, whereas high calcium levels stimulate FGF-23. Lastly, there is some evidence that FGF-23 also inhibits PTH secretion.
