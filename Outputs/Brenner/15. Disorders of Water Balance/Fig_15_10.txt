Fig. 15.10 Relationship between plasma arginine vasopressin (AVP) levels, urine osmolality, and plasma osmolality in subjects with normal posterior pituitary function (100%) compared with patients with graded reductions in AVP-secreting neurons (to 50%, 25%, and 10% of normal). Note that the patient with a 50% secretory capacity can achieve only half the plasma AVP level and half the urine osmolality of normal subjects at a plasma osmolality of 293 mOsm/kg H2O. However, with increasing plasma osmolality, this patient can nonetheless eventually stimulate sufficient AVP secretion to reach a near-maximal urine osmolality. In contrast, patients with more severe degrees of AVP-secreting neuron deficits are unable to reach maximal urine osmolalities at any level of plasma osmolality. (Adapted from Robertson GL: Posterior pituitary. In Felig P, Baxter J, Frohman LA, eds. Endocrinology and Metabolism. New York: McGraw-Hill; 1986:338-386.)
