Fig. 7.18 Mechanisms by which phosphate is transported across the proximal tubular cell. Apical uptake is mediated by the sodium-phosphate cotransporters lla and Ilc. The Na-K ATPase located at the basolateral aspect of the cell provides a sodium gradient within the cell that permits these sodium-phosphate cotransporters to take up sodium and phosphate. By binding to the NaPi-lla protein, NHERF-1 retains the NaPi-lla protein on the surface of the proximal tubular cell thereby enhancing phosphate uptake; phosphate uptake diminishes as a consequence of endocytosis of the NaPi-lla when it dissociates from NHERF-1, a process that is activated by the hormonal induction of protein kinase C and the phosphorylation of specific serine and threonine residues on the PDZ domain of NHERF-1. Ezrin, a protein that facilitates the association of NHERF-1 to the actin cytoskeleton also regulates proximal tubular phosphate transport. Ezrin knock down mice exhibit hypophosphatemia and osteomalacia and a reduction in NaPi-lla/SIc34A1 and NHERF-1 expression at the apical membrane of proximal tubules. The xenotropic and polytropic retroviral receptor (Xpr1) plays a role in the extrusion of phosphate at the basolateral cell surface. PT, Proximal tubule.
