Fig. 21.1 Public health implications of the burden of preterm and SGA births for 120 million births in countries of low and middle income in 2010. This figure is a pyramid diagram illustrating the loss of human capital associated with different categories of birth. The base of the pyramid represents the largest group with the lowest risk, and the top represents the smallest group with the highest risk. **Epidemiological category (from top to bottom):** *   **Top (Highest Risk):** *   Preterm SGA (LBW): 2.8 million *   Preterm AGA (LBW): 4-6 million *   **Second Level:** *   Term SGA (LBW): 10.6 million *   **Third Level:** *   Preterm AGA (not LBW): 6.3 million *   **Fourth Level:** *   Term SGA (not LBW): 19.0 million *   **Base (Lowest Risk):** *   Term AGA: 77.0 million **Associated Loss of human capital (from top to bottom):** *   Death *   Long-term disability and reduced learning potential *   Other long-term effects (e.g., stunting, higher risk of noncommunicable diseases) A side bar indicates the scale of the issue: *   Preterm "too soon" and/or SGA "too small" (43.3 million) *   LBW <2500 g (18 million) *   SGA and LBW (13.4 million) *   Preterm and LBW 7.4 million AGA, Appropriate for gestational age; LBW, low birth weight; SGA, small for gestational age. (From Lee AC, Katz J, Blencowe H, et al. National and regional estimates of term and preterm babies born small for gestational age in 138 low-income and middle-income countries in 2010. Lancet Glob Health.)
