Fig. 1.15 Ret signaling pathway. Ret is activated and becomes autophosphorylated on intracellular tyrosine residues upon association with glial cell-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) and GFR 1. Signaling molecules such as Grb2, Shc, FRS2, PLCy1, and Shp2 bind directly to the phosphorylated tyrosine residues within the intracellular domain of Ret. Recruitment of Shc, FRS2, and Grb2 leads to activation of ERK and PI3K/Akt pathways. GDNF-Ret signaling leads to the specific activation of a host of genes, some of which are strongly dependent on the upregulation of the transcription factors Etv4 and Etv5 (solid arrows). Etv4/Etv5 activation requires activation of the PI3K/Akt but not the ERK pathway. Sox8 and Sox9 are believed to act in parallel to reinforce transcriptional responses to GDNF-Ret engagement. Some of these pathways are shared with the FGF7/10-FGFR2 receptor signaling system. Spry1 and Spred2 negatively regulate ERK signaling, whereas Dusp6 likely mitigates the dephosphorylation of the Ret receptor, thus acting as part of a negative feedback regulatory loop.
