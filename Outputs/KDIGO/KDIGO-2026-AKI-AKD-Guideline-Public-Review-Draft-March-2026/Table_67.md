| Table 67 \| Determinants of the strength of recommendation Factors  Comment    Balance of benefits and harms  The larger the difference between the desirable and undesirable effects, the more likely a Level 1 recommendation is provided. The narrower the gradient, the more likely a Level 2 recommendation is provided.    Certainty of evidence  The higher the certainty of the evidence, the more likely a Level 1 recommendation is warranted. However, there are exceptions for which low or very low certainty of the evidence will warrant a Level 1 recommendation.    Values and preferences  The more variability in values and preferences, or the more uncertainty in values and preferences, the more likely a Level 1 recommendation is warranted. Values and preferences were obtained from the literature, when possible, or were assessed by the judgment of the Work Group when robust    Resource use and costs  evidence was not identified. The higher the cost of an intervention—that is, the more resources consumed—the less likely a Level 1 recommendation is warranted. |
