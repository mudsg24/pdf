| Table 40 \| Sick day medication guidance for adults Triggers to initiate sick day medication guidance: • Vomiting or diarrhea, resulting in significant fluid losses • Anorexia or nausea, resulting in significant decrease in fluid intake • New light-headedness, dizziness, or fainting, particularly with sitting or standing up • Decreased weight (3 kg in 2 days) • Decreased urine output When self-management is appropriate: • There is an absence of severe symptoms • Patients (and carers) feel they can cope • Patients can keep up with their fluid intake Medications that should be temporarily stopped (until symptoms are resolved or up to 3 days, whichever is earliest): • Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi)/angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) • Loop diuretics Thiazides/thiazide-like diuretics • Potassium-sparing diuretics • Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors • Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs • Metformin • If blood glucose is low, hold insulin/sulfonylurea/meglitinide until blood glucose recovers If blood glucose is more elevated than usual, empirical 10%–20% increase in basal and bolus insulin doses (if unsuccessful at lowering blood glucose, contact healthcare professional) • Angiotensin receptor neprilysin inhibitors • Direct rein inhibitors When assistance/support from healthcare professional should be sought: • Patients feel they are not coping • Signs and symptoms have not resolved within 72 hours • Patients cannot keep up with intake of foods or fluids • Patients have recurrent low blood glucose readings Patients experience significant increase in blood glucose not coming down with selfadjustment after 24 hours Adapted from Watson et al.<sup>690</sup> |
