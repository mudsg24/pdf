Fig. 4.1 Urinary Sediment Cells. (A) Isomorphic nonglomerular erythrocytes (diameter ∼6 μm). The arrows indicate the so-called crenated erythrocytes, which are a finding in nonglomerular hematuria. (B) Dysmorphic glomerular erythrocytes (diameter ∼6 μm). The dysmorphism consists mainly of irregularities of the cell membrane. Inset, Acanthocytes, with their typical ring-formed cell bodies with one or more blebs of different sizes and shapes. (C) Neutrophils (diameter ∼10 μm). Note their typical lobulated nucleus and granular cytoplasm. (D) Granular phagocytic macrophage (diameter ∼60 μm). (E) Different types of renal tubular epithelial cells (diameter ∼14 μm). (F) Two cells from deep layers of uroepithelium (diameter ∼18 μm). (G) Three cells from superficial layers of uroepithelium (diameter ∼25 μm). (Note the difference in shape, size, and ratio of nucleus to cytoplasm between the two types of uroepithelial cells. (H) Squamous epithelial cells (diameter ∼50 μm). (All images by phase-contrast microscopy; original magnification ×400.)
