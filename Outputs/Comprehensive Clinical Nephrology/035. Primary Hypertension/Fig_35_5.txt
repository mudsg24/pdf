Fig. 35.5 Hemodynamic Pathophysiology of Hypertension. Sodium retention induces increase in sodium concentration, hyperosmolarity, and rise in intravascular volume with an increase in cardiac output, possibly driven in part by the generation of cardiotonic steroids. This transient state is followed by sustained increment in peripheral vascular resistance and normalization of the cardiac output. These events result from the combined effects of immune (IL-17) stimulated increase in sodium reabsorption and stimulation of the sympathetic nervous system (SNS). These effects are counterbalanced by vascular endothelial growth factor type C–mediated increase in the lymphatic network and endothelial nitric oxide (NO) generation induced by intravascular dilation (see text). The increase in blood pressure tends to correct sodium retention by stimulating pressure natriuresis. CNS, Central nervous system; ENaC, endothelial sodium channel; NHE3, sodium-hydrogen exchanger 3; UNaV, urinary sodium excretion.
