### Fig_47_2

![Fig_47_2](Fig_47_2.png)

Fig. 47.2 Pathologic Features of Autosomal Recessive Polycystic Kidney Disease. (A) Cut section of autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD) kidney shows marked enlargement, preserved kidney configuration, and diffuse, radially arranged fusiform cysts extending from the medulla to the cortex. (B) High-power view shows the fusiform, radially oriented cysts in the cortex (hematoxylin and eosin [H&E] stain; ×10). (C) In liver with ARPKD (congenital hepatic fibrosis), the portal tracts are enlarged and uncharacteristically bridged by bands of fibrous tissue. The intrahepatic bile ducts are increased in number and ectatic, containing bile plugs (H&E stain; ×20).

### Fig_47_6

![Fig_47_6](Fig_47_6.png)

Fig. 47.6 The TSC1/TSC2 complex regulates mTORC1 signaling by integrating extracellular and intracellular signals that promote metabolic homeostasis. Under conditions that promote cell growth, the hamartin-tuberin complex is inactivated by Akt, leading to Rheb activation and the phosphorylation of S6 kinase (S6K) and 4E-BP1 by mTORC1. Conversely, energy deprivation activates AMPK, which phosphorylates tuberin, activating the hamartin-tuberin complex to inactivate Rheb and mTORC1, thereby inhibiting cell growth. mTORC1 inhibitors act by binding to FKBP12, which interacts with mTORC1 to inhibit downstream signaling. (Modified from Inoki K, Corradetti MN, Guan KL. Dysregulation of the TSC-mTOR pathway in human disease. Nat Genet. 2005;37(1):19–24.)

aMeckel-Gruber syndrome is a severe, often lethal, autosomal recessive disorder characterized by bilateral kidney cystic dysplasia, biliary ductal dysgenesis, bilateral postaxial polydactyly, and variable CNS malformations. The triad of kidney cystic disease, occipital encephalocele, and polydactyly is most common. Genes disrupted in Meckel-Gruber syndrome have also been identified in NPHP and Joubert patients, suggesting a phenotypic spectrum.

dJoubert syndrome is a genetically heterogeneous, autosomal recessive disorder characterized by developmental defects in the cerebellum (cerebellar vermis aplasia) and the eye (coloboma), as well as retinitis pigmentosa, congenital hypotonia, and either ocular motor apraxia or irregularities in breathing patterns during the neonatal period.
