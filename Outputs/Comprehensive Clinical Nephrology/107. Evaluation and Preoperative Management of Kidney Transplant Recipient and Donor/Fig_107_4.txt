Fig. 107.4 Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC) Crossmatch. (A) Recipient serum potentially containing donor-specific anti–human leukocyte antigen (HLA) antibodies is added to donor T or B lymphocytes, along with complement. (B) If donor-specific antibodies are not present, no lysis occurs and the result is deemed negative. (C) If donor-specific anti-HLA antibodies bind to the lymphocytes and then activate complement, cell lysis will occur and the crossmatch result will be deemed positive. The proportion of lysed cells is assessed, and the crossmatch is graded as being weakly, moderately, or strongly positive. (From Mulley WR, Kanellis J. Understanding crossmatch testing in organ transplantation: a case-based guide for the general nephrologist. Nephrology. 2011;16[2]:125–133.)
